Chapter I

Year Zero · The Calendar Begins

Kelvin & the Founding of Kelvopolis

Whatever brought him here, Kelvin arrived. He came with settlers and founded a city on the eastern shore of the continent with clear intention of permanence. The texts that survive from the early Kelvian period are administrative in nature — land grants, inventories, a partial census. They describe a functioning settlement, not a founding hero. He is referenced only as "the Founder." His decisions are recorded without explanation.

From Kelvopolis, settlers spread west and south, carrying their culture and technology to dwarven, elven, and halfling populations already living across the continent. Sarangshi, Sebrela, and Liebshire all attribute their founding to early Kelvian expeditions.[1]

[1] The current state of Kelvopolis ruins was surveyed by the Moriccian Geographic Commission in 1247 K.C. Their report, The Eastern Reaches: A Field Survey, documents substantial goblin and orc habitation across the original city footprint, with several structures in active use.

Chapter II

Year 50 – Year 433 · The Age of Roots

Expansion & Coexistence

The centuries following the founding of Kelvopolis were not ones of dramatic event. They were centuries of movement — of marriage, of trade routes slowly becoming roads, of roads slowly becoming towns. Human settlers spread west and south from Kelvopolis into a continent that was already inhabited: dwarves in the deep mountain ranges to the north and east, elves woven in the veins of ancient verdant forests, halflings along the fertile grasslands of their valleys.

The historical record for this period is rich in archaeology and thin in written text. What we can confirm is that coexistence was the norm, not the exception. Intermarriage between human settlers and local populations is evidenced in skeletal record, material culture, and the linguistic mixing present in the earliest formal documents of all three major polities. Dozens of small settlements rose and fell. Three endured long enough to become something more.

Year 0 · The Kelvian Calendar

The Founding of Kelvopolis

Kelvin arrives on the eastern shore of the continent with a group of settlers. The Kelvian Calendar begins. All subsequent dates are measured from this moment.

Years 50–300 · The Quiet Centuries

The Westward Dispersal

Settlers move inland and southward from Kelvopolis, intermingling with dwarven, elven, and halfling communities. Regional culture begins to differentiate along geographic lines. No single power dominates. Dozens of holds and small cities rise, trade, and disappear.

Years 300–420 · Three Cities Emerge

The Shape of Things to Come

Three regional centers grow from trading posts into genuine powers. Sarangshi rises from the southern steppes on overland trade and mountain pass control. Sebrela anchors the western coast on sea commerce. Liebshire, in the northern heartlands, becomes the continent's seat of both Lathanderian and Selunite tradition — the city whose fate will matter most in the decade to come.

Before Year 430 · Exact Date Disputed

The Burning of the Southern Forests

Sectarian tensions between the early Nakensia and Moricci regions over Selunite religious practices in Liebshire result in widespread arson across elven forest communities in the south. Thousands of elves are displaced. The land they held — which had served as a natural defensive buffer — is left open. This will matter within the decade.

Years 430–433 · The Invasion of the Tolkesh

Fire Over the Mountains of Alems

A coordinated force of orcs, giants, and goblins presses south and west through the mountain passes — not raiders, but a sustained campaign. The elven forest communities displaced years prior had served as a natural buffer. That buffer is gone. The Tolkesh drive deep into Alems, burning settlements and destroying agricultural infrastructure, and push into the western reaches of Nakensia. The three young city-powers face it largely alone.

Chapter III

Year 433 · The Defining Event

The Sun of Lathander

The single most consequential event in the recorded history of Mordis did not occur in a palace or a council chamber. It occurred in an open valley below the Braburg pass, during the worst military crisis the continent has ever faced. A wave of orcs, giants, and goblins — the historical record is consistent on scale; this was not a raiding party but a coordinated migration of force — pressed south and west through the mountain passes and across the open lands of northern Alems and Nakensia. The settlements of Liebshire, already weakened by the recent displacement of the elven communities who had served as their southern wall, faced destruction.

What happened at Braburg Valley is historically attested — the physical evidence of the valley itself is beyond dispute — though its mechanism remains the subject of ongoing theological argument. Nine paladins of the Order of Lathander, identified in contemporary accounts as the finest of their generation, gathered at Braburg and invoked something. The texts disagree on what to call it. They agree on what followed: the valley below the pass was rendered entirely to glass overnight. The advancing force did not retreat. It simply ceased to exist in any meaningful military sense. The nine paladins were not found among the living. Nor among the dead.

The valley was glass by morning. The Selunite elves who survived called it sacred ground. No scholar who has visited has been eager to disagree with them.

— Vane, A. Foundations and Fictions, Year 1198 K.C.

The political legacy of Braburg is as significant as the military one. The nine paladins are the founding ancestors of the ruling dynasties of Mordis. Their descendants hold corridors of power in all three major nations, and in the court of Eingarden. The Order of Lathander, built on their memory, remains one of the continent's most politically influential religious institutions — a fact that sits in complex tension with the Selunite communities whose survival it achieved.[2]

The Selunite Question

The faith of Selune holds that Selune and Lathander exist in balanced co-equality: the moon and sun are two aspects of the same divine order, neither subordinate to the other. The Order of Lathander — whose foundational myth involves a paladin invoking divine solar fire powerful enough to glass a valley — has historically been comfortable with readings that place Lathander at the center. The Selunite elven communities of Liebshire and the forests around Meselia are less comfortable. This tension is theological in its expression and political in its substance, and it has never been formally resolved. It periodically produces events that diplomatic correspondence describes, with careful neutrality, as "incidents of communal disagreement."

[2] For a full accounting of the dynastic lineages descending from the Braburg Nine, see Hartmann, L. Blood and Fire: The Dynastic Legacy of the Order of Lathander, Liebshire Press, 1231 K.C.

Chapter IV

Year 433 – Year 1261 · Eight Centuries of Consequence

The Long Centuries

The century following the Sun of Lathander remade the continent not through any single event but through the slow, merciless arithmetic of consequence. The Tolkesh invasion had emptied Alems of its mountain defenses and destroyed the agricultural communities that fed its east. The Holy Day had given the survivors a myth. What followed — famine, frost, territorial loss, and the grinding emergence of a lasting political order — built the world that now stands.

Years 433–480 · The Famine of Eastern Alems

The Nakensian Siphon

With eastern Alems' farming communities destroyed by the Tolkesh invasion, the region depends on food imports through Nakensian trade networks. Nakensia adjusts its pricing accordingly, diverting shipments to wealthier buyers while the east starves. The famine runs for decades. The distrust it produces shapes Alems foreign policy for centuries after.

Year ~620 · The Council of Eingarden

The Doctrine of Mutual Dependence

Tensions between the Order of Lathander and the Selunite communities reach a formal reckoning at Eingarden, the only city all parties will enter willingly. What emerges is not a resolution but a doctrine: that neither tradition can exist without the other, sun and moon inseparable. The political tensions do not disappear, but the Council establishes a vocabulary for coexistence that holds for centuries after.

Year 821 · The Freezing of the Strait of Luna’ara

The Kaerel Liberation

The Strait of Luna'ara freezes solid. Alems marches across and presses into Moriccian territory. Kaerel and Cosayv — Moricci's most productive client states — fall within a season. Alems releases both as independent republics rather than occupy them. Moricci loses its two most valuable northern outposts without a single naval engagement. It does not forget.

Years ~1180–1220 · The Island War

The Zaremian Succession Crisis

When the ruling line of a Nakensian vassal state above Zaremia collapses without an heir, Nakensia and Moricci both advance claims. Moricci wins not through force but through trade — outpricing and out-contracting Nakensia harbor by harbor until the island councils simply accept Moriccian terms. Two naval engagements are attempted and go badly. The treaty cedes the islands and several coastal towns to Moricci.

Year 1258 · The Tolkesh Siege of the East

The Dardan Expedition

A coalition of Lathandarian admirals launches an armada crusade from Thassy — north, then east — to relieve the Tolkesh siege of Kelvopolis and Andiuvanos. Twenty percent of ships are lost in the northern seas where the ocean meets the ice. Half the soldiers who fought at Andiuvanos do not return. On the southern passage home, where the ocean meets the magma, eighty percent of the remaining fleet is lost. No survivor has ever spoken of what they encountered there. In formal Lathandarian records, the expedition is simply listed as having occurred.

Chapter V

The Modern Age · Year 800 to Present

The Powers of Mordis

Eight centuries of war, diplomacy, plague, famine, religious schism, and dynastic maneuvering have produced a continent in permanent, uneasy equilibrium. Three great powers dominate. A fourth sits at the center of all of them, claims to control nothing, and quietly holds everything together.

Moricci

King Charles · Western Seaboard

Naval PowerTradeTheology

The dominant maritime power on the continent. Moricci controls the western sea lanes through naval strength and centuries of mercantile calculation, with sharp class divisions between its merchant elite and a substantial slave class at the base. Its current foreign policy is defined by the question of Lis — pirate chains to the west that embarrass Moriccian claims to oceanic dominance. That question appears close to resolution.

Nakensia

Queen Kati · Southern Reaches

MilitaryMagicCultural Diversity

Forged through centuries of conquest and absorption, Nakensia is a nation of deep cultural diversity and persistent internal tension — proud of what it contains, constantly negotiating between central authority and the autonomy of its constituent peoples. Its military tradition is without peer on the continent.

Alems

King Unther · Northern Plains

PopulationAgricultural WealthBulwark

The most populous nation on Mordis, stretching across the northern plains from the Lunara'a strait to the eastern grasslands. Its wealth is agricultural; its vulnerability the same. When the strait freezes, armies walk across it. Alems absorbs the first shocks so that others do not have to.

Eingarden

King Ivan · The Central Sanctum

DiplomacyDynastic LegitimacySpiritual Authority

No great army, no great territory — only the only legitimate neutral ground on the continent. King Ivan is uncle to Charles of Moricci, brother to Unther of Alems, and nephew to Queen Kati of Nakensia. When the three powers need to stop fighting without appearing to back down, Eingarden is the table. Ivan is exceptionally good at setting tables.

The Present Balance

The current political moment favors Moricci. The island war of recent decades, in which Nakensia ceded significant holdings above the Zaremia coast, shifted the naval balance decisively westward. The earlier Lunara'a freezing and subsequent Alems incursion into Morician territory left relations between those two powers in managed tension. The Republics of Kaerlel and Cosayv, diplomatically released from Morician authority within living memory, remain economically dependent on Morician sea lane access. And a transaction of uncertain but potentially significant character recently took place aboard a Drow captain's vessel in the western approaches — its terms are unknown to all three courts, and its consequences have not yet become apparent.

Chapter VI

Year 1261 · The Present Day

The Voyagers

In the twelve hundred and sixty-first year of the Kelvian Calendar, a small vessel departed the western ports under royal commission, tasked with crossing the western expanse. Its complement was not assembled for heroism. It was assembled for utility: a navigator, a survivalist, a demonstration of physical prowess, and an administrator whose name carried more institutional weight than his current condition might suggest to a casual observer.

They have crossed. Where they are now is, by definition, beyond the reach of this account. What they left behind is a continent in motion — old alliances recalibrating, old grievances resurfacing, and at least one agreement made in circumstances that none of the courts on Mordis yet knows about.

Halfling Ranger · Survivalist

Belnan

Lunara'a · Alems Border Territory

For many years he was a fixture of the trade roads between Lunara'a and Eberne — a caravan guard with a reputation for bringing travelers home intact. A fresh rabbit for the hungry, a deer at the water's edge for the exhausted, a swift and quiet end for those who preyed on both.

Blood becomes an easy thing to spill when its in the service of your own.

Then something found him on those roads. Not something that ambushed him — something that recognized him. The roads went dark in the years that followed, and the travelers who disappeared along them were not accidents. When Belnan came home, the roads were quieter. He does not explain why. He carries only what he cannot put down and the secrets he now must keep.

The darkness becomes harder to escape the more one sets out to play the role it hungers for.

Elven Warlock · Navigator

Galicus

Meselia · Liebshire · Steins Gate Observatory

His published argument — that the world turns around the sun, not the moon — attracted sufficient theological complaint that his family's safety in Liebshire could no longer be taken for granted. Amidst this, Selune has granted him providence.

The more he stares into the night sky, the more it seems to stare in return.

He has spent his life at the edge of what is known, but he didn't expect the sky to close in. What is taking shape at the periphery of his observations is not something he can categorize, and the entity attending his work is not one he can name. He is not afraid of what he doesn't understand. He is afraid of what he may see.

You may always look into the unkown, but the void will always be staring back.

Dwarf Fighter · Champion

Fogguki

Mines of Uroy · Mines of Tabra'an

His mother is an oracle of the Mines of Uroy, father the general-king of the Mines of the Tabra'an — a title won in blood, by tradition, since the lineage began. Fogguki was created, indoctrinated, and forged to be next.

He is the strongest person most people have ever encountered. His father made sure he knows that is not sufficient.

The succession fight is singular by tradition: two men enter, one man leaves. Fogguki entered the arena against his closest friend, Tarak, had him pinned and stood up. The title passed as he walked away, and with it his name in his father's house. He was sooner offered to the foreign crown rather than be acknowledged as a son who chose mercy over legacy. All too familiar with the lucha, the liberador sets out to conquer a ring of his own.

Every ache and weakness a mortal reminder that he will one day prove his father right .

Tiefling Monk · Administrator

Mark

Aquenta · Studied in Fries · Served in Sebrela

Undefeated in court, renowned for knowing when someone in the room was righteous — a gift of divine vision he believed was Lathander's. An infernal twist of fortune tested his judgement and led him to wake from a coma without his sight or his sister.

He is working out what to do with a faith that was never wrong.

The trial exists in adjacent documents, with all its details expunged from text and memory — only his tiefling blood left as a testament. He still carries the title of Late Great State Magistrate, and now serves as the Right Hand of the King. He travels in search of a reason that does not require him to stop believing in the faith that repeadt leaves him broken.

He is afraid his gift never failed. That Lathander saw this coming and called it good.

The Three Constellations

Bokeby

The Tortle scholar's gift to Galicus was, on its surface, a children's picture book: simple illustrations of three star formations, labeled in a script that does not match any currently catalogued writing system. Each constellation, correctly identified in the night sky, points toward one of three cities somewhere to the west of the western expanse. The book was written for children, the Tortle explained, because adults look at stars and see what they expect to see. The cities are not named in the book. They will have to be found.
